## 6. Voice & Content Rules (Zero-Tolerance)

Pulled into their own section because these are the rules that should apply automatically, every time, regardless of which event or format is being written. Inherited from Digital First Productions' own standard, held even more strictly here given the confidentiality stakes of a therapy practice:

1. **Never** use negation-into-affirmation structures ("no es X, es Y" or any asymmetric variant where one half negates and the other affirms the same subject)
2. **Never** end on a question as the CTA — the close is always a concrete action ("reserva tu lugar," never "¿te animas?")
3. **Never** give exactly three examples in a list — use two, four, or more
4. **Avoid** em dashes in copy
5. **No** manufactured slang or forced casualness — natural rhythm over performed informality
6. **No** invented client stories, no invented testimonials, no claims without a source
7. **Zero** identifiable client details, ever, in any post, caption, or script
8. **One** clear next step per piece, never competing links or CTAs
9. Ground every psychological or mechanistic claim (like the Reiki-before-Constellations sequence) in what's actually been confirmed, not general wellness-industry assumptions

## 8. Content Formats That Work

**Interview format** (proven across multiple scripts): one strong hook question that makes the mechanism or the stakes immediately felt, two follow-up questions that go deeper or surface something specific and real (like what Peggy said she loved about the first Reiki Sistémico), then a closing answer that folds the CTA in naturally rather than tacking it on. Works better than a straight monologue because the questions do the work of pacing and curiosity that would otherwise need forced transitions.

**Caption format** (promotional mode, per the authentic-content-writer skill): ground the pitch in one real, specific mechanism or feeling rather than general enthusiasm. The Reiki-before-Constellations sequence is the anchor almost every caption should return to. Close with a single, concrete CTA line: event name, date, facilitator credential, "Reserva tu lugar. El link está en mi bio."

**Variation sets:** when asked for multiple versions of the same core message, vary the entry point (a question, a statement, a personal reflection from Peggy, a description of the body's state) while keeping the same underlying mechanism and the same CTA. Genuinely different angles, not five rewordings of one angle.
